# Design research, 30 October 2026

Three surveys run in parallel before the second design direction was written. Each is the researcher's report as delivered, lightly trimmed of its working notes. The synthesis is in `../design-direction-v2.md`.

- `preparedness-sites.md`: how government, NGO and humanitarian preparedness sites look and explain readiness (UK Be Ready, Ready.gov, Red Cross, NZ Get Ready, Canada, Sweden MCF, Finland 72tuntia, Tokyo Bosai, Australian RediPlan, Germany BBK, Sphere, UNHCR, Norway DSB, Netherlands Denk Vooruit).
- `energy-backup-calculators.md`: how consumer backup products explain "how long will this keep my things running", with sourced UK appliance draws, source capacities and food-safety horizons (Tesla, Enphase, EcoFlow, Bluetti, Jackery, Anker, Generac, Ford, Indra, Wallbox, Octopus, GivEnergy, myenergi, Sunsave, Energy Saving Trust).
- `cinematic-web-design.md`: what makes a screen read as cinematic yet usable (Territory, Perception, Hansen, Linear, Awwwards and Information is Beautiful winners, Dark Sky, Met Office, Windy, Ordnance Survey, the Shipping Forecast, Field Notes, Finisterre, National Trust, the Economist, The Pudding, Apple, Stripe Press) and three candidate directions.

The three findings that shaped the direction:

1. **Nobody shows progress.** Every preparedness site is a checklist, a quiz or a per-person table. Not one has a meter, a timeline or a kit that visibly fills. The Board and the strip are an open field.
2. **The sites that feel trustworthy own one colour, sit on a warm neutral, picture the home rather than the disaster, and repeat one number.** Norway's self-preparedness week already uses "the light from a lantern against a dark circle" as its mark; the lantern is the right object.
3. **The best "what stays on" explainers model watts times hours a day per appliance and show the first thing that falls off**, with the fridge-4-hours and freezer-48-hours marks as real horizons.
