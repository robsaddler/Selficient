# Threat model: the flood map lookup, and a plan that fits its answers

*12 November 2026. Rob asked why the wizard asks about a tree and not a flood plain. It did ask, as the last question on the form, defaulting to "I have not checked yet", and an answer left there produced nothing. The plan now looks the postcode up on the nation's own flood map, says what it found whatever it found, and sends a household to its own nation's services. The same change makes the plan's rules read the later questions (the kind of car, a stove, a water butt, infants, pets), and lets the wizard be reopened with its answers in place.*

## What it is

- **A port, `IFloodRiskProvider`**, asked by `PlanHouseholdHandler` only when the household left the flood question at "Look it up for me". It answers the highest band within 25 metres of the postcode's centre, which water that band is about, and whose map it is; or a failure.
- **Three adapters**, each a typed HTTP client with a fixed base address from `UpstreamOptions` and the standard resilience pipeline:
  - England: the Environment Agency's NaFRA2 map service, two feature-info requests (rivers and the sea; surface water).
  - Wales: Natural Resources Wales's FRAW on DataMapWales, three feature requests (rivers; the sea; surface water and small watercourses).
  - Scotland: SEPA's open flood map service, three to nine count requests, low likelihood first.
  - Northern Ireland has no service that may be asked and no licence to reuse, so it has no adapter. A postcode there is a failure, by design.
- **A cache** (`CachedFloodRiskProvider`): a band for thirty days, a failure for ten minutes, keyed by nation and coordinates rounded to four decimals.
- **`FloodServices`**: each nation's "check your address" and "sign up for warnings" addresses, constants in the Domain, carried on an action as an `ActionLink`.
- **`wizard.js`**: on `/plan/start`, fills the form from the profile the browser already keeps.

## Accepted

- **The middle of a postcode, 25 metres round it.** The Office for National Statistics places a postcode's centre on a real address, so the box is one property and its near neighbours. A wider box would call most streets in Britain high risk, because surface water is mapped in patches a few metres across; a single point would miss the patch next door. The household's own check of its own address is the better answer, and every sentence says so.
- **Ground in no polygon is very low.** Each map covers its whole nation, so this is the maps' own meaning, but it is our reading of them and not a sentence any of the three agencies publishes.
- **The Environment Agency's service is a map service, not a documented lookup API**, and it throttles hard. At launch volumes some lookups will fail and those households will be asked to check for themselves. The answer that removes the limit is to hold the open data ourselves, in SQL Server, refreshed on a schedule. That is not built.
- **Northern Ireland is not looked up.** Its map is to look at, not to ask, and commercial reuse needs a licence from Land & Property Services.
- **SEPA calls its services beta.** If they move, Scotland degrades to "check for yourself" until the adapter is updated.
