# Threat model: whose home it is, what it is connected to, and who would need help

*13 November 2026. Rob asked for the five things the last review found still missing. A tenant was being offered rooftop solar, a boiler socket and an arborist; a house on a borehole was told to ring its water company; a household on a prepayment meter, or with someone who could not get out alone, got nothing for it; and a third-floor flat in a flood zone was sold air-brick covers. The wizard now asks seven more closed questions and the plan, the board and the upkeep follow them.*

## What it is

- **Seven answers on the form**, all closed: whether anyone would need help to leave (a tick), whether the home is owned or rented (a list of two), a cellar (a tick), a bed on the ground floor (a tick), where the water comes from (a list of three), a tank instead of a sewer (a tick) and a prepayment meter (a tick). No free text.
- **`Circumstances`** in the Domain carries them, with the flat's floor, onto `HouseholdProfile`. `Circumstances.Create` refuses a choice off the list and a floor outside 0 to 40.
- **Three new rules** (`HelpToLeaveRule`, `PrepaymentRule`, `OwnSuppliesRule`) and changes to seven existing ones. Every sentence is a constant chosen by the answers; nothing a household typed is ever in one.
- **The board**: a home on its own water has its own tap fixture (pumped, which stops with the power unless a whole-house battery carries it; or fed by gravity, which does not stop), and a home with its own tank is not on the sewers, so stored water still flushes when they stop.
- **The upkeep calendar's address** carries three more yes-or-no facts: `meter`, `supply`, `tank`.

## A stairlift, and what sleeping bags are worth (added the same day)

Rob's grandmother reaches her bed and her bathroom by a stairlift. The wizard now asks "Is there a stairlift?" with four answers: no; yes, with its own battery; yes, and it stops with the power; yes, and I do not know which. The board has a row, "Getting upstairs", on the Survive rung, only for a house that has one.

Accepted with it: the two days a battery lift is scored for is one maker's statement (Acorn: more than ten return journeys, about two days at four or five a day), read on its site on 13 Nov 2026, and the energy its charger draws is our estimate, not a published figure. The action tells the household to get its own lift's number.

## Accepted

- **A treatment plant with a pump is treated as a tank.** The form asks one question about drains, so a pumped system is not sold the toilet liners although it stops with the power. The action it gets says so and tells the household to ask whoever empties it how long it can go. A second question would settle it; one was judged enough.
- **A private supply's own failures are not a scenario.** The board's "water main bursts" scenario does not touch a home on its own supply, which is true, and there is no scenario for a failed pump, a frozen pipe or a dry spring. The risk sentence names them.
- **A flat's floor is said twice**, once to the plan (`Circumstances.Floor`) and once to the board (`ResilienceProfile.Floor`), both from `PlanForm.FloorOfFlat`.
- **The fire and rescue visit has no link.** There is no single national address for it that we have opened and can stand behind for all four nations, so the sentence says to ask the local service.
