## 2. Your eight questions, answered

### Q1. A month of tinned food and water; keep the bath full; big bottles in the garage?

**Food: yes.** A month of food for four is cheap and has no downside if you rotate it. Use the "store what you eat, eat what you store" rule: buy an extra of each tinned or dried thing you already use until the cupboard holds four weeks, then always take from the front and restock at the back. Budget roughly £150-250 one-off, then nothing extra. Include tinned meat, fish, beans, soup, veg, fruit, rice, pasta, oats, UHT milk, long-life juice, peanut butter, biscuits, and a cooking fuel (section 7). Two weeks of anything that needs no cooking at all. Pet food if relevant.

**Bath full at all times: no.** It is not drinking water (bath, pipework and open air contaminate it), it goes stale and grows biofilm in days, it takes a bathroom out of use, it is a drowning hazard, and refilling weekly wastes about 100-150 litres plus the gas to heat it if you ever use it. Two better options:

- **WaterBOB** bath liner, £35-39. A food-grade 380-litre bag you lay in the bath and fill from the tap in 20 minutes when a storm warning or outage notice comes. Keeps 16 weeks. This gives you the "bath of water" idea without any of the drawbacks.
- A **garden water butt** (200 litres, £50-80) off a downpipe for toilet flushing and washing. Never drink it untreated.

**Big containers in the garage: yes, but 25-litre not "massive".** Four 25-litre food-grade jerry cans with taps (£10-18 each) give 100 litres, which is drinking water for four for about 8 days at 3 litres each. A single 200-litre barrel cannot be lifted or rotated. Fill from the tap, add Aquatabs (£8 per 100) so you can extend rotation to 12 months, keep them dark and off concrete, and swap the water at the clock changes. Keep 3 days (36 litres) of sealed shop bottled water separately, which lasts 2 years and is what you would reach for first.

**For a month** you do not need 360 litres stored. You need 100-130 litres stored plus the means to make more: a **British Berkefeld gravity filter** (6 litre, £160) will turn the water butt, a stream or rain into drinking water indefinitely at 70+ litres a day. That is the real month-long answer and it costs less than the extra cans.

**Verdict:** 36 L bottled + 4 x 25 L cans + WaterBOB + Aquatabs + Berkefeld. About £300 total. Add a water butt for flushing.

### Q2. Crank charger and several small power banks for phones?

**Power banks: yes, two or three.** Ugreen Nexode 25,000 mAh 145 W, £47-60 on sale, or Anker 25K 165 W, £75-79. Each charges a phone four to five times. Keep them at 50-80% and top up every three months. About £100-150 for the household.

**Crank: only as part of a radio.** Tests show a minute of cranking gives roughly a minute or two of phone use. Solar or crank "power banks" at £25-45 are novelties with inflated capacity claims. Skip them.

**Radio:** the Roberts SolarDAB 99 (£150) covers DAB+, FM, crank, solar, USB-C and is IP55. A cheaper pairing is the Roberts Play 20 DAB+/FM on AA cells (£80) plus a Philips TAR1609 FM crank radio with torch (£36). Keep 12 lithium AAs either way; AA cells are the real backup, the crank is the bonus. BBC Radio Wales and Radio 4 carry emergency information.

**Verdict:** £100-150 of power banks plus £120-150 of radio.

### Q3. Pedal-powered chargers for bigger batteries?

**No.** A fit adult sustains 75-150 W for half an hour to an hour. One hour of hard pedalling makes 50-100 Wh, which is one phone charge for the four of you or 2-3% of a 2 kWh battery. The K-Tor Power Box is a US import at roughly £230-400 landed and delivers 20-50 W. A £109 100 W folding solar panel out-produces an hour of pedalling even on a dull Welsh winter day and costs a third as much. Only consider pedal power for a long siege indoors with no sun, which is not a scenario worth paying for.

### Q4. Portable solar battery, camper-van style?

**Yes. This is the single best mid-budget purchase for this house**, and it stays useful for camping, the car and the garden whatever you do about rooftop solar.

All current units are LiFePO4 with 3,000-6,000 cycle life and pure-sine output, and they act as a UPS so the router, fridge or boiler never notices the cut.

| Size | Recommended | Price seen Oct 2026 | What it runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 kWh | Bluetti Elite 100 V2 (1,024 Wh, 1,800 W) | £469-499 (Bluetti UK sale); Jackery 1000 v2 £398 at Currys; EcoFlow Delta 3 Classic £490 at Costco | Phones, lights, router and boiler for a day; fridge-freezer 8-14 h |
| 2 kWh | **Bluetti Elite 200 V2** (2,074 Wh, 2,600 W) | £849 Amazon low, £999 Bluetti UK | Fridge-freezer 14-17 h plus boiler 10-20 h of firing; the family-of-four sweet spot |
| 4 kWh | EcoFlow Delta Pro 3 (4,096 Wh) or Anker Solix F3800 | £2,300-2,800 | Multi-day cuts; only sensible if you will not fit rooftop solar with backup |

Panel: Bluetti PV200 200 W (£249) or EcoFlow 220 W bifacial (£297-349). In Nov-Feb a 200 W panel yields perhaps 200-400 Wh a day here, so it tops up rather than sustains. In summer it fully recharges the 2 kWh unit in a day or two.

**The boiler needs an electrician visit.** UK boilers are hard-wired to a fused spur. Ask an electrician to fit a plug-and-socket on the boiler's spur (if the boiler manual allows) or a small changeover switch, about £100-200. Then in a cut you plug the boiler into the power station and have heating and hot water. Never backfeed a wall socket.

**Decision rule:** if you intend to fit rooftop solar with battery backup within 12 months, buy the 1 kWh unit and a 200 W panel (about £720) because the house battery will carry the big loads. If rooftop solar is two years or more away, buy the 2 kWh unit (about £1,100-1,250 with panel).

### Q5. £1,000 cash at home?

**Reasonable, with two caveats.** Card terminals, ATMs and online banking all die with the power or network, so cash is the only thing that works at the Tesco 400 m away in a cut. The government's checklist says "cash", and Storm Darragh showed shops going cash-only.

- **Check your contents insurance cash limit first.** Most policies cap cash at £250-£1,000 and the common figure is £500. Keep at or under that, or declare more.
- **Small notes.** £500-£1,000 in £5, £10 and £20 notes plus a bag of £1 and £2 coins. Nobody will change a £50.
- Keep it in a 30-minute fire-rated box (Sentry 1210 £44, Master Lock L1200 £51) hidden, not in the bedroom drawer. Put copies of documents in the same box.

**Verdict:** £500 in small notes is enough for a week of fuel and food; go to £1,000 only if your policy covers it.

### Q6. Rechargeable LED lights instead of candles?

**Yes, and the single most useful item is the plug-in power-failure light.** Xtralite NiteSafe plug-in torches (£20 each) sit charging in a socket and switch on automatically the instant the power drops, so nobody is on a dark stair. In a three-storey split-level house, stairs are the injury risk. Put one in every hallway and stairwell and one in the kitchen: 4-5 units, £80-100.

Then:
- **Headtorches x4**, Petzl Tikkina (£17-26 each), ideally the Hybrid version that takes AAAs as well as a rechargeable core. Hands-free light is worth more than lanterns.
- **Lanterns:** one Ledlenser ML6 (£68-90, 750 lm, 200 h on low, doubles as a power bank) for the living room and a Ledlenser ML4 (£36) per occupied bedroom.
- Spare lithium AAA/AA cells for the ones that take them.

**Verdict:** about £250 and nobody is in the dark for a week.

### Q7. Candles and matches?

**Keep a few, but treat them as third-line.** A box of tea lights in glass jars, a couple of pillar candles and three lighters plus a box of matches costs under £15 and never goes off. Fire risk is real in a house with children and timber decking outside, so candles only on a hard surface, never on the stairs, never left burning in a bedroom. Matches have a second job: if your hob is gas, it still works in a power cut when lit by hand. Keep a long-reach lighter by the cooker.

### Q8. Rooftop solar with a large battery?

**Yes, this is the one item that pays for itself and also delivers the resilience, but only if you specify backup.** See section 3 for the full workings. Summary:

- Your front roof faces about south-west and yields about 936 kWh per kWp a year, within 5% of a due-south roof. The rear faces north-east and has three dormers, so it is not worth panelling.
- The front slope and the gable's south-east face can probably take **5-6.5 kWp** (11-14 panels at 455 W). A 7-8 kWp system would need the rear or the garage roof. An installer survey settles this.
- Pair it with a **13.5 kWh battery with whole-home backup** (Tesla Powerwall 3 with Backup Gateway, or Sigenergy SigenStor with Sigen Gateway). Standard inverters shut down in a power cut by law (anti-islanding), so without a gateway your "large battery store" gives you nothing on the night you want it.
- **Budget £14,000-17,000** at 0% VAT for 5.5-6.5 kWp plus a 13.5 kWh battery with backup gateway on a three-storey roof. Get it invoiced before **31 March 2027** when VAT returns to 5%.
- **Yes, you can charge it at night on cheap electricity and run the house on it by day.** Without an EV the best fully eligible tariff is EDF Empower Fixed at 10.36p overnight (01:00-04:00) and 20.22p by day versus the 26.55p cap; Octopus Flux would be better again if it reopens to new customers.
- **Expected saving £1,200-1,500 a year, payback 10-13 years**, near-zero bills April to September, and in a power cut the fridge, boiler, lights, router and most of the house keep running for a day or more while the panels recharge the battery.
- **Property value:** studies range from +1-2% (Solar Energy UK) to +6-7% (Swansea University 2024). Treat it as a bonus not a return, and never sign a roof-lease "free solar" deal, which hinders mortgages.
- **Finance:** Green Homes Wales (Development Bank of Wales) offers 0% loans of £1,000-£25,000 over up to 10 years for owner-occupiers with no means test; register early because the fund is limited. Switch Together South East Wales (the former Solar Together scheme) includes Caerphilly and runs group-buying rounds; register interest for the next round.
- **Avoid GivEnergy.** The company entered administration in April 2026 and warranties are not being honoured.

## 10. Decking, leaf litter and garden fire

The garden backs onto the tree belt and the open field. South Wales valleys see grass and hillside fires most dry springs and summers, usually started deliberately or by barbecues. Leaf litter under timber decking is the classic ember trap.

- Clear under the deck every autumn after leaf-fall and again in late spring. A leaf blower through the gaps works; if the boards are screwed, lift two and rake. Consider fine wire mesh around the deck skirt to stop future build-up.
- Keep a metre or so of gravel, paving or bare earth between the back fence and anything that burns (deck, shed, fence panels, log store).
- A hose that reaches the back fence, on an outside tap, with a spray nozzle.
- No disposable barbecues on or near the deck. A garden fire blanket (£20) hung by the back door.
- Store the cooking gas cartridges and any petrol in the garage, not under the deck.

## 11. The tall tree

A storm-felled tree is far more likely to cost this household than any national-level scenario, so it goes near the top of the list.

1. **Get an arborist survey**, not a prune by default. An Arboricultural Association approved contractor will assess species, health, lean, root plate and target (your house) for £100-250. A sound tree with a good root plate often needs little work. Heavy topping weakens many species and can make them more dangerous as weak regrowth develops. The usual recommendation is a crown reduction or crown thinning, £400-1,500 depending on size and access.
2. **Establish who owns it.** If it stands in the tree belt behind you it belongs to the developer, a management company, the council or a neighbour. A dated letter from you noting the risk puts the liability on them.
3. **Check protection.** Ask Caerphilly County Borough Council whether the tree carries a Tree Preservation Order or sits in a conservation area; work on a protected tree needs consent and unauthorised work is a criminal offence.
4. **Tell your insurer in writing** if the arborist flags a risk, so a later claim cannot be refused for a known hazard. Buildings cover normally pays for storm damage from a falling tree.
5. **Use the same visit** for advice on the tree belt as a fire and leaf-litter source.
