# UK "Be Ready" campaign — briefing

*Compiled 29 October 2026 from UK news coverage of the preceding six weeks.*

## What was actually said, and when

- **22 October 2026, the "Be Ready" campaign launched.** The Cabinet Office replaced GOV.UK/Prepare with gov.uk/beready and a new three-day checklist. Security Minister Dan Jarvis announced it by written statement, framing it as "everyday national resilience" and saying there was no "specific thing" the public was being asked to prepare for. ([ITV](https://www.itv.com/news/2026-10-23/what-items-are-on-the-be-ready-checklist-and-how-can-households-get-prepared), [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uk/2026/10/23/uk-government-advises-stockpiling-water-and-food/))
- **The checklist:** 3 litres of drinking water per person per day for three days, food needing no fridge or cooking, torch and spare batteries, battery or wind-up radio, charged power bank, cash in notes and coins, blankets and warm clothing, first aid kit and medicines, copies of documents, a written list of phone numbers, and supplies for babies and pets. The site says "From severe weather to cyber attacks, emergencies can disrupt the services you rely on." ([beready.campaign.gov.uk](https://beready.campaign.gov.uk/), [Prepared Britain item-by-item](https://preparedbritain.co.uk/guides/government-preparedness-campaign))
- **Jarvis's stated reason:** "By taking simple steps to be ready for everyday emergencies like severe storms, people can reduce pressure on emergency services." He cited last winter's storms and this summer's wildfires. ([Emergency Services Times](https://emergencyservicestimes.com/2026/10/23/be-ready-campaign-aims-to-reduce-pressure-on-emergency-services/))
- **"Stockpile" is the press's word, not the government's.** The campaign never uses it. The headlines trace to Defence Secretary Wes Streeting telling The Sun on 26 September that households should have "some cash at home, having a supply of bottled water indoors, having some tinned goods", which he called "a simple ask". ([Prepared Britain](https://www.preparedbritain.co.uk/guides/uk-government-stockpile-food-water))
- **Streeting on Question Time, 22 October,** defended it as "really practical things" for "everyday threats" like flooding, and said he wanted people "alert not alarmed". An audience member accused him of scaremongering. Shadow Security Minister Alicia Kearns called it "lecturing the public". ([Brit Brief](https://britbrief.co.uk/politics/westminster/streeting-blasted-for-scaremongering-stockpile-warning.html))
- **25 October, Streeting in the Telegraph:** Russia could be "in a position to attack" by 2030, so the armed forces have "a little over 1,000 days" to be ready. This is about military readiness with allies, but it landed three days after the household advice and is why the two have fused in people's minds. ([Jerusalem Post summary](https://www.jpost.com/international/article-912745))
- **Public reaction:** Iceland reported a 40% rise in tinned food sales, Maplin said torch sales nearly quadrupled and radio sales almost doubled in three days, and water and beans sold out at several retailers. The British Retail Consortium warned "panic buying is quite easy to generate". A disaster psychologist at the University of Lancashire called the advice "realistic, quite low level, and should be reassuring", and PA found "no evidence the advice has triggered mass panic". ([The National, 27 Oct](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uk/2026/10/27/beans-water-and-radios-sell-out-after-uks-stockpile-warning/), [PA via AOL](https://www.aol.co.uk/articles/realistic-reassuring-uk-public-responding-172218000.html), [SWNS](https://discover.swns.com/2026/10/panic-buyers-leave-supermarket-shelves-empty-after-govs-be-ready-campaign/))

## Why it is being said now

Two separate tracks that the media has merged.

**Track one is civil resilience, and it is old.** The Prepare campaign launched in May 2024 under the previous government with the same three-day advice. The July 2026 Resilience Action Plan promised a public campaign "later this year". The 2026 National Risk Register lists 95 risks; the ones driving this checklist are mundane: storms, floods, power cuts, water outages and cyber incidents against companies and utilities. The government's own backdrop statement lists "new conflict in the Middle East, a growing number of sub-threshold attacks from Russia, the unprecedented growth of new technology, several major cyber attacks, and increasingly severe weather". ([Prepared Britain](https://www.preparedbritain.co.uk/guides/uk-government-stockpile-food-water), [NRR 2026 guide](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a50d68d9e9c95844ae64caf/A_Guide_to_the_2026_National_Risk_Register.pdf))

**Track two is defence planning, and it has sped up.** The Telegraph reported on 25 August that the Cabinet Office pulled parts of the home defence programme forward from 2035 to 2030, is rewriting the 22-year-old "war book", and will run the largest home defence exercise in decades, Operation Albiston Shadow, in 2027. The trigger is the assessment that Russia's rebuilt forces and war economy could threaten NATO by 2030, plus a run of grey-zone incidents: Russian-ordered arson on Ukraine-linked warehouses in London, a GRU spy ring jailed, the spy ship Yantar loitering over undersea cables, and an Iranian cyber attack that reportedly shut a British power station for four days. ([Telegraph via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/civil-defence-planning-accelerated-over-181600699.html), [Global Situ](https://www.globalsitu.com/post/between-peace-and-war-the-uk-russia-threat-landscape-in-2026), [Prepared Britain, "Is the UK preparing for war?"](https://www.preparedbritain.co.uk/guides/is-the-uk-preparing-for-war))

The honest reading: ministers wanted the household campaign out before the 2027 exercise and the defence budget rise, and the hostile-state backdrop is real. But the Chief of the Defence Staff said in December 2025 there is "only a remote chance of a significant direct attack or invasion by Russia on the UK". The checklist protects against a power cut from a storm or a cyber attack on a water company, which happen in Britain every year. Nothing has been issued about shelters, evacuation, conscription or mobilisation.

## What this means for a household

The advice is cheap and sensible on its own merits. A few bottles of water, some tins, a torch, a wind-up radio and some cash is a storm kit, not a war kit, and can be built over a few normal shops. Also register with energy and water suppliers' Priority Services Register if anyone in the household is elderly, disabled or dependent on powered medical equipment; that is who the three-day buffer is designed to free help for.
